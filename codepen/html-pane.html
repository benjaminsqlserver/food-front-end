Our story

One woman, one pot, and a street that got busier around her.

In 1998 Bashirat Adeyemi sold rice and stew from a bench outside her brother's shop on Ajayi Crowther Street. She had one pot, one bag of rice and a rule she has never broken: nothing leaves the kitchen that she would not put in front of her own children.

Victoria Island grew up around her — glass towers, banks, traffic that does not move. The bench became a counter, then a dining room, then the room upstairs. The pot is still the same. So is the rule.

Everything here is cooked to order, in the open, by cooks who trained under her. The yam is pounded by hand each morning because a machine makes it gummy. The jollof sits over firewood until the bottom catches, because that is where the flavour lives.

The table

Eat in, take away, or let us bring it to you

Four things we get right, every day, whether you are eating upstairs or on a Zoom call two streets away.

  • Cooked to order

    Nothing sits under a lamp. Your soup is ladled when you order it, which is why we say 20 minutes and mean it.

  • Heat you choose

    Every dish carries a heat rating, and every one of them can be made milder. Ask. Nobody here is offended by a mild ayamase.

  • 45-minute delivery

    Across Victoria Island, Ikoyi and Lekki Phase 1 for a flat ₦1,500. We send a rider, not a courier who has never held a soup flask.

  • The room upstairs

    Twenty-four seats, one long table, and a door that closes. Book it for a birthday, an owambe overflow, or a very serious meeting about rice.
